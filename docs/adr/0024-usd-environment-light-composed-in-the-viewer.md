# The usd view's environment light is composed in the viewer, and its skies and HDRIs are fetched on first use, never committed

Task SPEC-31. Extends #148 (the fallback dome and auto exposure for stages not lit for RTX). Amends the offline rule (`docs/contracts.md` section 14: every app asset is vendored at build time, with the named network exceptions): the usd view's environment assets are fetched at run time, on first use, as ADR 0019 did for the html view's pages.

## Context

Stages without lights of their own, or lit only for another renderer, look wrong in the usd view: #148 gave them a flat default dome. Users want a real sky behind and a sun's shadows, with the time of day, the clouds and the haze in their hands, a ground to stand the stage on, or an image-based light, without touching the stage file (the view is read-only, ADR 0007 and the usd design). Two assets are at hand: NVIDIA's dynamic skies from the public Omniverse sample assets (seven stages, each a `DomeLight` bound to an MDL material with a `DistantLight` sun rig; six are one procedural material, `ProceduralSky.mdl`, with different arguments; the seventh is textured, `SunsetSkyMat.mdl`; every one references a preview scene with a floor and spheres), and the five Poly Haven HDRIs of the 3d view (CC0). Only RTX compiles MDL; Storm cannot. The repository is public and MIT; an installed app has no web build.

## Decision

1. **The params: `environment` (`stage`, `sky`, `hdri`), `sky` and `hdri` (the names), `time_of_day`, `cloudiness` and `haze` (the sky's controls, null for the sky's own value) and `ground`.** The usd view keeps them across stages, the VIEW panel's controls and the agent set them (one user submit per change; a slider moves the sky live and submits on release), `snap` and `describe view:usd` report them (`hdri`'s choices are the 3d view's manifest's, not a list of their own), and what shows is the source's answer, with a note when it is not the choice.
2. **Composed in the viewer's session, never written to the file.** Under RTX the environment is a reference added to the inline root's own scope (`/__specto/Environment`) through ovstage's `add_usd_reference_from_string`, swapped at runtime in one commit with a history reset, the way the collision overlay is; the sky's controls are attribute writes on that reference's prims (the material's inputs, the sun's transform), no reload. Under Storm it is a sublayer of the session layer (`specto_app.usd.environment.author`). Both renderers put it at the same path. Nothing is authored over the stage's own prims: the stage's own lights stay as they are under both renderers, and the environment adds to them.
3. **The skies and the HDRIs are fetched on first use into the user's Specto data folder, never committed.** The NVIDIA Omniverse License Agreement grants a "non-exclusive, non-transferable, non-sublicensable" licence to "Distribute Content (unmodified or as modified by you) as incorporated into your products or services for the purpose of enhancing your work, subject to the distribution requirements below, but not deploy or distribute the Content on a stand-alone basis" (2.1), and forbids copies "in any publicly accessible software repositories" (6.4) and any use that makes them "redistributable at no charge" (6.9). Files in an MIT repository would break all three. So `specto_app.usd.skies` downloads them from NVIDIA's public bucket when the Sky choice is first used: each file pinned by sha256 and size, written to a `.part` that is cut off past its pinned size and checked before it is moved into place (a mismatch is removed and refused; offline is said in words), under a lock file on the folder so two Specto processes never write the same file, on a worker thread with its progress under the panel's control, the sky asked for first, then every other sky once (the six procedural ones, about 1 MB each with the preview scene, then the textured CloudySky with 67 MB of textures last), into `<user dir>/skies` (`specto_core.instances.user_dir`: the folder the app's settings live in, `SPECTO_USER_DIR` overrides it) with the licence notice beside them, never refetched once cached. The HDRIs come the same way from Poly Haven into `<user dir>/hdri`, pinned by the 3d view's manifest (`web/3d/environments.json`), unless the web build's cache in a source checkout already has them. `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` section 3 records the terms.
4. **One `.usdz` per sky, with its MDL inside.** The fetch packs each sky's files into a stored zip with the sky stage first, as the usdz format asks; ovrtx resolves `<name>.usdz[Dynamic/<name>.usd]` and compiles the MDL from inside it (measured 2026-11-03: the same frame as from a plain folder, at the cost of six resolver warnings while it looks for its standard modules inside the package first). The plain files stay under `skies/src/` beside the packages, shared between skies. The first step after a sky comes in blocks on its MDL compile for about ten seconds and draws black: the source keeps stepping past it and says so in the phase label.
5. **The sky's controls go through the material's own maths.** The time of day sets the procedural material's `TimeOfDay` with `SunPositionFromTOD` (its sun-position function, `suncalc`, from the sky's own latitude, longitude and day of the year) so the dome's sun moves as the asset's authors meant; the viewer places its own `DistantLight` by the same maths, ported (`environment.sun_angles`), and turns the asset's rig light off, because the rig's rotations are the Sun Study extension's work in Kit, not the asset's. Cloudiness maps 0-1 onto `CloudCoverage` -0.5 to 1.0 (the material thresholds its noise at 0.5 minus the coverage; the skies author -0.24, 0.01 and 0.74) with `CumulusEnabled` off at 0; haze maps 0-1 onto the material's `haze` 0-20 (it adds 2 and saturates its effects by about 17). The textured sky's material has no sun maths of its own (its stage authors `SunPositionFromTOD` and `TimeOfDay` on the shader, but `SunsetSkyMat` declares no such input and never reads its `TimeOfDay`): the viewer computes its `Elevation` and `Azimuth` by the same ported maths, maps cloudiness onto its `CloudBias` 3 to -2 (its soft range, higher is fewer clouds) with `CloudsEnabled` off at 0, and offers no haze, which it lacks. A null control leaves the sky's own value, read from its files.
6. **The ground is the preview scene's floor.** Every sky stage references `Sky_Elements/PreviewScene.usda`, a floor cube (10 x 20 m, its top at height 0) and fifteen spheres; the fetch keeps it and the sphere it references in each sky's package, and `params.ground` (off by default) references the scene from the sky's package under the environment with its spheres inactive, scaled from its centimetres to the stage's units. It shows with a sky; under Storm, where the sky falls back to an HDRI, there is no ground.
7. **Fallbacks are a step down each, said in words.** Storm, or a sky not fetched yet, shows the HDRI for the sky (the panel shows which HDRI stands in, and its list to pick another); an HDRI not fetched yet shows the stage's own light. The note goes to the panel, `snap` and the agent; while an asset is on its way the note is the fetch's progress, and a failed fetch its reason.
8. **Exposure follows the environment.** The procedural skies and the HDRI dome (Kit's default intensity, 1000) are on RTX's photometric scale, so a stage without lights keeps the fixed exposure while an environment lights it and its fallback dome is hidden; a stage with plain UsdLux lights of its own keeps #148's auto exposure, and so does the textured sky, which washes out under the fixed exposure (measured). Both flip at runtime with the choice (a bool write on the viewer's camera, a `visibility` write on the viewer's dome).
9. **Orientation and intensity are each renderer's own.** The skies are authored Z-up, ovrtx keeps a dome's pole along +Z and Storm along +Y whatever the stage's up axis (measured 2026-11-03), so a Y-up stage turns the sky and ovrtx's dome by -90 degrees about X (after the sky's own rotation, which stays in the xform order) and a Z-up stage turns Storm's dome by +90. The HDRI dome is 1000 under RTX (Kit's scale) and 0.5 under Storm, which has no tone mapping.

## Consequences

- The first Sky pick on a machine needs the network for about a second (and a minute more in the background for the textured sky), the first HDRI pick on an installed app for a second; the panel says so, and says when it cannot. Nothing is fetched until the user picks Sky or HDRI, and nothing again once cached.
- The stage file is never dirty: `Usd.Stage` under Storm has the environment in an anonymous layer, ovstage under RTX in its own scope, and a stage saved from elsewhere never sees either.
- A stage with lights of its own keeps them under every choice: the environment is for stages that have none, and adds to those that do. A Kit-saved stage that already carries a sky gets a second one under `sky` or `hdri`.
- The GPU tier grows by one RTX flow; the environment's look is checked there and in `test_usd_environment_ovrtx.py`, not offscreen.

## Considered options

- Commit the skies to the repository: rejected on the licence quoted above.
- A developer tool (`tools/fetch_*.py`) into the build root: rejected by the owner for a runtime fetch into the user's own data folder, so a packaged Specto needs no build step for it and every user's copy is their own; the HDRIs follow for the same reason.
- A plain folder instead of the `.usdz`: the files stay beside the package, but the view references the package, one file to point at.
- Reload the stage with the environment in the inline root: rejected; `--fresh-renderer` would start a new renderer per switch, a second or more and a loading label, where a reference swap is one commit and a slider move one attribute write.
- Drive the asset's own sun rig (`AxisSHA`, `AxisDeclination`) instead of the viewer's light: rejected; ovstage flattens xform ops at population and the rig's authored angles do not match the material's maths anyway (Kit's extension computed them), so one light placed by the ported `suncalc` keeps the shadows and the dome's sun together.
- Under Storm, hide the stage's own lights while the HDRI dome lights it (the first cut, since Kit-authored lights wash Storm's image out, #143): rejected in review; the feature lights stages that have no lights, it does not replace the lights a stage has.
