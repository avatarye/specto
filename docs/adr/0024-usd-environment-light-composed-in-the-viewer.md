# The usd view's environment light is composed in the viewer, and NVIDIA's skies are fetched on first use, never committed

Task SPEC-31. Extends #148 (the fallback dome and auto exposure for stages not lit for RTX).

## Context

Stages without lights of their own, or lit only for another renderer, look wrong in the usd view: #148 gave them a flat default dome. Users want a real sky behind and a sun's shadows, with the time of day, the clouds and the haze in their hands, or an image-based light, without touching the stage file (the view is read-only, ADR 0007 and the usd design). Two assets are at hand: NVIDIA's dynamic skies from the public Omniverse sample assets (seven stages, each a `DomeLight` bound to an MDL material with a `DistantLight` sun rig; six are one procedural material, `ProceduralSky.mdl`, with different arguments; the seventh is textured), and the five Poly Haven HDRIs the 3d view already fetches (CC0). Only RTX compiles MDL; Storm cannot. The repository is public and MIT.

## Decision

1. **The params: `environment` (`stage`, `sky`, `hdri`), `sky` and `hdri` (the names), `time_of_day`, `cloudiness` and `haze` (the sky's controls, null for the sky's own value).** The usd view keeps them across stages, the VIEW panel's controls and the agent set them (one user submit per change; a slider moves the sky live and submits on release), `snap` and `describe view:usd` report them, and what shows is the source's answer, with a note when it is not the choice.
2. **Composed in the viewer's session, never written to the file.** Under RTX the environment is a reference added to the inline root's own scope (`/__specto/Environment`) through ovstage's `add_usd_reference_from_string`, swapped at runtime in one commit with a history reset, the way the collision overlay is; the sky's controls are attribute writes on that reference's prims (the material's inputs, the sun's transform), no reload. Under Storm it is a sublayer of the session layer (`specto_app.usd.environment.author`). Both renderers put it at the same path. Nothing is authored over the stage's own prims except, under Storm's HDRI, `visibility` overs that hide the stage's own lights, in that session sublayer, as the collision mode already does with gprims.
3. **The skies are fetched on first use into the user's Specto data folder, never committed.** The NVIDIA Omniverse License Agreement grants a "non-exclusive, non-transferable, non-sublicensable" licence to "Distribute Content (unmodified or as modified by you) as incorporated into your products or services for the purpose of enhancing your work, subject to the distribution requirements below, but not deploy or distribute the Content on a stand-alone basis" (2.1), and forbids copies "in any publicly accessible software repositories" (6.4) and any use that makes them "redistributable at no charge" (6.9). Files in an MIT repository would break all three. So `specto_app.usd.skies` downloads them from NVIDIA's public bucket when the Sky choice is first used, each file pinned by sha256 (a mismatch is removed and refused; offline is said in words), on a worker thread with its progress under the panel's control, the sky asked for first, then every other sky once (the six procedural ones, about 0.3 MB each, then the textured CloudySky with 67 MB of textures last), into `<user dir>/skies` (`specto_core.instances.user_dir`: the folder the app's settings live in, `SPECTO_USER_DIR` overrides it) with the licence notice beside them, never refetched once cached. `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` section 2.5 records the terms. The HDRIs are the `.hdr` files the web build already downloads for the 3d view; nothing is duplicated.
4. **One `.usdz` per sky, with its MDL inside.** The fetch packs each sky's files into a stored zip with the sky stage first, as the usdz format asks; ovrtx resolves `<name>.usdz[Dynamic/<name>.usd]` and compiles the MDL from inside it (measured 2026-11-03: the same frame as from a plain folder, at the cost of six resolver warnings while it looks for its standard modules inside the package first). The plain files stay under `skies/src/` beside the packages, shared between skies.
5. **The sky's controls go through the material's own maths.** The time of day sets the material's `TimeOfDay` with `SunPositionFromTOD` (its sun-position function, `suncalc`, from the sky's own latitude, longitude and day of the year) so the dome's sun moves as the asset's authors meant; the viewer places its own `DistantLight` by the same maths, ported (`environment.sun_angles`), and turns the asset's rig light off, because the rig's rotations are the Sun Study extension's work in Kit, not the asset's. Cloudiness maps 0-1 onto `CloudCoverage` -0.5 to 1.0 (the material thresholds its noise at 0.5 minus the coverage; the skies author -0.24, 0.01 and 0.74) with `CumulusEnabled` off at 0; haze maps 0-1 onto the material's `haze` 0-20 (it adds 2 and saturates its effects by about 17). A null control leaves the sky's own value, read from its files. **No ground switch:** the material always draws its own ground below the horizon and exposes no parameter for it, and the preview ground that ships beside the skies is a separate stage, not part of any sky.
6. **Fallbacks are a step down each, said in words.** Storm, or a sky not fetched yet, shows the HDRI for the sky; an HDRI not built shows the stage's own light. The note goes to the panel, `snap` and the agent; while a sky is on its way the note is the fetch's progress, and a failed fetch its reason.
7. **Exposure follows the environment.** The skies and the HDRI dome (Kit's default intensity, 1000) are on RTX's photometric scale, so a stage without lights keeps the fixed exposure while an environment lights it and its fallback dome is hidden; a stage with plain UsdLux lights of its own keeps #148's auto exposure. Both flip at runtime with the choice (a bool write on the viewer's camera, a `visibility` write on the viewer's dome).
8. **Orientation and intensity are each renderer's own.** The skies are authored Z-up, ovrtx keeps a dome's pole along +Z and Storm along +Y whatever the stage's up axis (measured 2026-11-03), so a Y-up stage turns the sky and ovrtx's dome by -90 degrees about X (after the sky's own rotation, which stays in the xform order) and a Z-up stage turns Storm's dome by +90. The HDRI dome is 1000 under RTX (Kit's scale) and 0.5 under Storm, which has no tone mapping.

## Consequences

- The first Sky pick on a machine needs the network for about a second (and a minute more in the background for the textured sky); the panel says so, and says when it cannot. The HDRI needs `packages/specto-web/build.py` to have run; the panel says which is missing.
- The stage file is never dirty: `Usd.Stage` under Storm has the environment in an anonymous layer, ovstage under RTX in its own scope, and a stage saved from elsewhere never sees either.
- A Kit-saved stage that already carries a sky gets a second one under `sky` or `hdri`; the stage's own lights stay under RTX (only Storm hides them, since its headlight stood in for them). Turning the stage's own lights off under RTX is a later decision.
- The GPU tier grows by one RTX flow; the environment's look is checked there and in `test_usd_environment_ovrtx.py`, not offscreen.

## Considered options

- Commit the skies to the repository: rejected on the licence quoted above.
- A developer tool (`tools/fetch_*.py`) into the build root: rejected by the owner for a runtime fetch into the user's own data folder, so a packaged Specto needs no build step for it and every user's copy is their own.
- A plain folder instead of the `.usdz`: the files stay beside the package, but the view references the package, one file to point at.
- Reload the stage with the environment in the inline root: rejected; `--fresh-renderer` would start a new renderer per switch, a second or more and a loading label, where a reference swap is one commit and a slider move one attribute write.
- Drive the asset's own sun rig (`AxisSHA`, `AxisDeclination`) instead of the viewer's light: rejected; ovstage flattens xform ops at population and the rig's authored angles do not match the material's maths anyway (Kit's extension computed them), so one light placed by the ported `suncalc` keeps the shadows and the dome's sun together.
- Storm with the stage's own lights plus the dome: rejected; Kit-authored lights (a 5000 DistantLight) wash Storm's image out (#143), which is why Storm lights with a headlight; the dome replaces the headlight, and the stage's lights stay out as before.
